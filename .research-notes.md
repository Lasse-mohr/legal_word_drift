## 2026-05-11 — EuroVoc-driven cross-period APD pipeline (scaffold)

Built the per-label drift pipeline under `scripts/eurovoc_drift/`. New
`src/paths.py` (`Paths` frozen dataclass, exposed as `PATHS`) is now the
canonical path map; `src/utils/config.py` is unchanged so older scripts
keep working.

Selection (`01_select_labels.py`): a label-year is "present" iff it has
≥10 corpus hits; keep labels with ≥10 such years; drop EuroVoc domain
roots (`microthesaurus_id` null). Yields **1,671 labels** (1,013 pref +
658 alt) across 22 domains — Law 223, EU 156, Social 134, Trade 116,
Business 111, Finance 109, Education 108, Geography 108. Token lengths:
1=885 / 2=563 / 3=145 / ≥4=78 (max 10). 12 labels have a
microthesaurus_id but no `domain_name` — upstream parsing quirk in
`concepts_enriched.csv`; they group into "(unassigned)" in the plots.

Indexing (`02_index_label_usages.py`): chunks labels into batches of
500, compiles whitespace-tolerant case-insensitive regexes
(`re.escape` + `\s+` between words, `\b…\b`), scans paragraphs. Smoke
test on 1990–1991 ran in ~20 s/year; full sweep ≈ 12 min.

Embedding (`03_extract_embeddings.py`) reuses the existing BERT encoder.
The encoder's `extract_embedding` mean-pools all subwords whose offsets
fall inside the matched char span — multi-word labels are handled
automatically (one 768-d vector per occurrence covering every subword
of the phrase). N=100 usages/year.

Heatmaps (`04_compute_heatmaps.py`) reuse
`temporal_drift.compute_cross_period_table` with `min_usages=10`,
`min_years=10`, `max_per_year=50`. Output joined with selection
metadata for domain-aware ranking.

Plots (`05_plot_per_domain.py`) — one heatmap-grid + marginals-grid +
drift-excess histogram per EuroVoc domain, plus a global top-K for
cross-domain context. 2003–04 corpus-volume anomaly left in the year
axis (documented but not patched).

**Parking lot:**
- *Per-concept aggregation* (pool pref+alt usages of the same concept
  into a single year-bucket of embeddings before computing the
  Y×Y matrix). Currently per-label only. Per-concept would maximise
  per-cell N at the cost of synonym-mixing; useful as a follow-up
  comparison to ask whether synonyms of the same concept track each
  other or diverge.
- *Subsample cap* held at 50; revisit if heatmaps look noisy.
- *Whitespace-tolerant lowercase normalisation*: indexer collapses any
  internal `\s+` to a single space and lowercases before checking
  membership, so corpus occurrences with newline-wraps still match the
  EuroVoc label key.

## 2026-05-11 — EuroVoc × corpus coverage probe

Ran `scripts/eurovoc/02_corpus_coverage.py` over the 1990–2025 paragraph
JSONLs. Tokenises each EuroVoc label (pref + alt) with the corpus tokenizer
(`preprocess_paragraph`) and counts contiguous-token matches per year.
17,356 labels in, 14 empty after stopword removal, 31 dropped (>6 tokens).
Length histogram: 1-token 3.7 k, 2-token 9.6 k, 3-token 3.0 k, longer 943.

Headline numbers (per concept, pref ∪ alt aggregated):
- 5,708 / 7,523 concepts (76 %) have at least one corpus hit.
- 3,186 (42 %) are present in ≥10 distinct years — the slice that is
  actually viable for a per-year drift signal.
- Total hits dominated by Law (3.0 M), followed by Education, Politics,
  Business and competition, Trade (~650–740 k each).

Domain shape (concepts present in ≥10 years / concepts total):
- Strong: Social questions 280/695, Law 268/440, Finance 233/471,
  EU 210/460, Trade 199/350, Business & competition 166/244.
- Weak: Science 32/138, International orgs 56/234, Energy 66/167.
- Geography 233/819 is middling-by-rate but big in absolute terms
  (country names are the bulk).

Top-100 concepts (full table in `coverage_top100.md`) are dominated by
generic legal terms hitting 36/36 years: *decision, regulation, directive,
case-law, State, appeal, national, judgment, court of justice, …* — the
expected ranking and a sanity check that the pipeline works.

Quirks noted:
- 21 of the top entries are EuroVoc **domain roots** (e.g. "12 LAW",
  "legal science", "social affairs") rather than leaf concepts — they
  live in the special `domains` scheme and carry no microthesaurus tag
  (`domain_name=nan` in the table). Easy to filter via
  `microthesaurus_id is not null` when we select drift candidates.
- Years **2003 (33 k hits) and 2004 (44 k)** are anomalously low against
  the 150–650 k range of surrounding years. Likely a corpus-volume
  issue in those JSONLs (not a script bug — `regulation` shows the
  same dip). Worth checking paragraph counts before including 2003–04
  in any year-pair drift sweep.

Outputs in `data/processed/eurovoc_coverage/`:
- `label_year_counts.parquet` — raw (concept_id, label, label_type, year, count), 120 k rows
- `concept_coverage.parquet` — per-concept totals, per-year & per-decade vectors, metadata
- `coverage_by_domain.csv`, `coverage_by_decade.csv`
- `coverage_top100.md` — the markdown report (top 100 ranked by years_present, then total_count)

## 2026-05-08 — EuroVoc vocabulary: structure and parsed dump

Replacing the ad-hoc / frequency-sampled probe vocabulary with EuroVoc
(English, full hierarchy). Parsed `eurovoc-skos-ap-eu.rdf` (456 MB SKOS-XL
RDF/XML) with `scripts/eurovoc/01_parse_eurovoc_skos.py` — single streaming
pass via `xml.etree.iterparse`, ~9 s.

EuroVoc structure (three levels):

1. **Master scheme** `100141` "EuroVoc" — every concept belongs to it.
2. **127 microthesauri** — thematic groupings whose English labels carry a
   4-digit prefix, e.g. `1216 criminal law`, `2841 health`. The first two
   digits identify one of **21 domains** (`12` = Law, `28` = Social
   questions, etc.). Concepts attach to a microthesaurus via
   `skos:inScheme`.
3. **`domains` scheme** — separately lists the 21 domain roots.

IDs are globally unique across the dump (concepts, labels, schemes,
notes all live in the same `eurovoc.europa.eu/N` namespace) — no
collisions, so resolving `100278 → "7211 regions of EU Member States"`
is purely for readability.

Format note: SKOS-XL stores labels as first-class resources with one
`xl:literalForm` per language; we keep only `xml:lang="en"`. Of 420 k
total label literals, 17.5 k are English.

Outputs in `data/processed/eurovoc/`:
- `concepts.jsonl` (7,523) — full graph + micro/domain tags
- `concepts_enriched.csv` — flat per-concept table
- `schemes.csv` (129) — scheme id → label, domain code, microthesaurus name
- `labels_en.csv` (17,530) — pref + alt labels, one per row

Domain coverage: 138–819 concepts per domain (Geography largest, Science
smallest). Law domain has 440 concepts / 336 leaves — the most directly
relevant slice for CJEU judgments, but coverage of judgment-corpus
vocabulary is still an empirical question.

Next: tokenise EuroVoc labels, intersect with the BERT corpus vocab,
report coverage per domain/microthesaurus. Decide whether to filter to
single-token labels for the existing per-word drift pipeline, or to
extend embeddings to multi-word terms.

## 2026-05-06 — PCA top-component removal does not change AUC

Removing the top 0/1/2/3 PCs (per-word, fit on pooled-year embeddings, then
re-normalised) on the 4-word pilot moved mean AUC by < 0.05 in either
direction and left the permutation null on `organization` at ≈ 0.49 across
all k — so the C2ST drift signal does not live in the dominant
common-direction subspace, and we drop PCA pre-processing from the pipeline.
See `scripts/bert/29_linear_probe_pca_removal.py`.

## 2026-05-04 — Linear-probe AUC as a per-word drift statistic (calibration)

Pivoting away from monosemy gates. New per-word drift statistic: for each
consecutive year-pair, fit L2 logistic regression on L2-normalised BERT
embeddings labelled by year, report held-out AUC. This is a per-word C2ST
(Lopez-Paz & Oquab 2017); appears novel for diachronic LSCD — closest prior
work is Liu et al. 2021 (permutation test on cosine distance, not a learned
classifier).

Calibration on `organization`, `general`, `citizen`, `referring` (script 27,
70/30 split, class-balanced, 10-perm year-label null on `organization`):

- Permuted null mean AUC = **0.493** — well-calibrated, no high-dim
  data-piling inflation in this configuration.
- Real means: citizen 0.75, organization 0.65, general 0.56, referring 0.56
  — citizen and organization comfortably above the floor.
- Corpus-composition confound still untested; a matched control word is the
  cleaner check.

Next: extend the sweep to the full pilot vocabulary (the cross-word AUC
distribution is the main descriptive object) and bump permutations to
~200–1000 per pair so each (word, year-pair) gets its own MC p-value.
Parked: k-fold agreement, coefficient-vector inspection.

## 2026-04-21 — Monosemous drift: strict definition finds nothing; need to back up

Ran the monosemous pipeline (scripts 23–25) end-to-end. The per-year pairwise-distance gates (dip-test p-value + usage-count floor) do what they're supposed to — they isolate a cohort that behaves genuinely monosemously. Within the years where we have enough observations to estimate the within-year distance distribution robustly, these terms do **not** drift.

So under this very strict definition, monosemous legal terms appear stable across 1990–2025. Clean negative result, but also a dead end for the original question.

The motivation was different: words like *referring*, *organization*, *citizen* showed strong block structure in cross-year cosine distances between per-year mean embeddings — suggestive of regime shifts. The strict monosemy filter excludes exactly that kind of word (they're polysemous enough to fail the dip test in several years).

Next: back up. Reconsider the selection criterion — we want words whose *cross-year* structure is informative, not words that are monosemous every year. Likely means re-centering the analysis on the block-structure signal from the mean-embedding distance matrix, and treating dip-test monosemy as a descriptive covariate rather than a gate.

## 2026-04-21 — Monosemy classifier thresholds

Explored `dip_by_word_year.csv` in `notebooks/01_monosemous_word_exploration_21-04-2026.ipynb`.

Observation: many words look monosemous most years, but a few low-`n` years drag p down and kill a fraction-based rule.

Classifier: a year is *evaluable* iff `n >= N_FLOOR`, *passes* iff `p > P_THRESHOLD` AND `dip < DIP_CEILING`. Word is monosemous iff `n_pass >= K_PASS` AND `n_fail <= MAX_FAILS`.

One-knob sensitivity (defaults N=150, p=0.1, dip<0.02, K=5, fails=0):

    max_fails > k_pass > n_floor > p_threshold >> dip_ceiling

`dip_ceiling` ≈ no-op → likely drop. `max_fails` is the dominant knob — policy choice, not statistical.

Next: decide `max_fails`, wire selected (word, year) set into script 24.
